We submit our manuscript entitled "Single-Cell Characterization of Malignant-Myeloid Interaction Transcriptomic Features Identifies a Dual-Circuit Adenosinergic Signaling Hypothesis in Colorectal Cancer" for consideration in PeerJ.

This study characterizes the transcriptional programs governing malignant-myeloid crosstalk in colorectal cancer and proposes a candidate mechanistic hypothesis — a dual-circuit adenosinergic signaling axis — as a framework for understanding immune evasion in the myeloid-enriched CRC microenvironment. Using single-cell RNA-seq data from three CRC cohorts, we constructed a 10-gene transcriptomic framework (CRC-MMIS) through WGCNA and Cox-LASSO regression, trained on TCGA-COADREAD (N=431) and evaluated across five external GEO cohorts (total N=1,122) plus an independent RNA-seq cohort (CPTAC COAD, n=105). CellChat-based ligand-receptor analyses (86 interactions across 24 pairs) provided orthogonal support for the malignant-myeloid communication model.

Three findings define the contribution. First, this study presents, to our knowledge, the first CRC transcriptomic framework constructed with explicit single-cell-resolved malignant-myeloid interaction guidance, supported by permutation-based ligand-receptor analyses in GSE146771 and validated in an independent PD-1-treated cohort (GSE205506). Second, it identifies a coordinated dual-circuit adenosinergic transcriptional program — ENT1 (SLC29A1)-mediated adenosine import in tumor cells and ENTPD1/2-NT5E-mediated extracellular adenosine generation in the myeloid compartment — as a testable hypothesis for future investigation. Third, exploratory TIDE-inspired analysis suggests that MSS patients may be stratified for differential predicted immunotherapy benefit (MSS-Low 69.5% vs MSS-High 49.5%, p=3.5×10⁻⁵), a finding that warrants prospective validation.

All analyses were performed with R 4.6.0 for statistical modeling (survival, glmnet, WGCNA) and Python 3.13 for single-cell processing. Data and code will be deposited on Zenodo upon acceptance, consistent with PeerJ data availability requirements. All references have been verified against PubMed.

This manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the submission.
